# Landing Page Design Research

Breakdown of four reference landing pages in the sales/agency/coaching niche.
Analyzed from full-page screenshots — font identifications are educated guesses,
and some small copy (especially the French page) is below readable resolution.

---

## Reference 1 — WCC Certified (Daniel G / World Class Closer)

**Category:** Direct-response VSL + inline order form. Old-school, high-pressure.

### Palette
- Canvas: near-black with a deep navy cast (`#0B0E1A`-ish), shifting to pure
  black in lower sections
- Accent: a single hot red (`~#E63329`) — the ONLY color on the page
- Text: white headlines, gray-400 body
- Hero uses a dark-overlaid photograph (stage/event crowd shot) rather than flat color

### Typography
- Display: very heavy condensed uppercase grotesque (Anton / Druk / Archivo Black
  family). "WCC CERTIFIED" runs nearly edge to edge — the headline IS the hero.
- Body: neutral sans, gray, generous line height
- Eyebrows: tiny red uppercase with wide letter-spacing
- Highlighted words inside headings turn red (`THE **ONE** THING`, `WHY **7 DAYS**
  IS ALL YOU NEED`)

### Section-by-section
1. **Nav** — logo left with red mark, two center links (WHAT YOU GET, FULL
   PROGRAM), red CTA pill right (START FREE TRIAL). Minimal, only 2 links.
2. **Hero** — giant headline + a circular red/black "World Class Closer"
   certification seal overlapping the top-right of the headline. The seal is the
   brand device; it reappears as certificates later.
3. **VSL** — "WATCH THIS TWO-MINUTE OVERVIEW VIDEO BELOW" with red play marker,
   centered video with oversized red play button
4. **Offer card (above the fold-ish)** — this is the page's whole engine. A dark
   panel containing:
   - "WHAT YOU'RE GETTING" / "7 DAYS FREE" with a struck-through anchor price
   - 5 benefit rows, each a red circular checkmark + bold uppercase title + gray
     description (value stacking)
   - "TOTAL VALUE $10,000 → 7 DAYS FREE", then "Then only $297/month"
   - Inline form: Full Name, Email, phone w/ country-flag selector, industry
     dropdown, legal consent checkbox
   - Full-width red submit: "SECURE YOUR SPOT, 7 DAYS FREE"
   No separate checkout page. Conversion happens in the hero.
5. **"THE ONE THING"** — 3 stacked cards with a red left-border accent on
   indigo-tinted panels (Closing / Promoting / Coaching), closing italic line
6. **"WHY 7 DAYS IS ALL YOU NEED"** — two paragraphs of long-form copy with bold
   emphasis, then a photo of three physical certificates fanned out (making a
   digital product feel tangible), then a red pill CTA
7. **Testimonials** — horizontal drag carousel, 5 cards visible with the last one
   bleeding off the right edge to signal scrollability. Red circular avatars.
8. **"WHO'S THIS FOR?"** — 3×3 grid of 9 qualifying cards, each with a small red
   icon, uppercase title, one-line description. Casts an extremely wide net.
9. **Full-red CTA block** — the page's only bright section. Total contrast
   inversion, black button on red. Impossible to scroll past.
10. **Footer** — links, then a wall of tiny earnings-disclaimer legalese

### Mobile
- Headline scales down hard but stays the dominant element; the seal likely
  shrinks and repositions above or beside the wordmark
- The offer card becomes full-width and very tall — the form alone is 4 fields +
  consent + button, so the hero probably runs 2.5+ screens before the fold ends.
  This is normal for direct response but it's a lot of scroll before any proof.
- 3×3 grid → 1 column = 9 stacked cards, a long dead scroll. 2-column would be better.
- Testimonial carousel → single-card swipe, works well
- The red CTA block is the strongest mobile moment on the page
- Disclaimer wall becomes an enormous grey block at the bottom

### Verdict
Highest-pressure of the four. Effective at what it does, but it reads as
"internet marketing 2019." The value stack and struck-through pricing signal
*discount*, not *premium*.

## Reference 2 — French Shopify Coaching ("Lance ta boutique rentable")

**Category:** Editorial founder-led coaching page. The best-designed of the four.

### Palette
- Canvas: true black, with a faint blue perspective grid receding in the hero
- Accent: electric blue (`~#2E7FFF`) — again a single accent
- Borders: 1px `rgba(255,255,255,0.08)` — subtle, everywhere
- Soft blue radial glows behind key elements

### Typography — the signature move
Every heading is **heavy uppercase sans + italic serif**:
- "LANCE TA BOUTIQUE" (caps) + "*rentable.*" (italic serif)
- "RÉSULTATS" + "*réels.*"
- "TU POSTES." + "*Rien ne vend.*"
- "3 PILIERS." + "*[Zéro] magie.*"
- "DES RÉSULTATS." + "*Pas des promesses.*"
- "ZÉRO" + "*théorie.*"

Repeated on literally every section heading. It creates a rhythm you feel
scrolling even if you don't read French. This is the single most transferable
idea from these four pages.

Eyebrows are tiny letterspaced caps flanked by dot/diamond markers.

### Section-by-section
1. **Nav** — almost nothing. "LA VISION" left, "RÉSERVER" right. Extremely confident.
2. **Hero** — eyebrow, mixed-type headline with an inline Shopify bag emoji,
   caption, video with blue play button (podcast/plant setup thumbnail),
   blue pill CTA
3. **Stat pair** — +270 élèves / +3M€ generated, big blue numerals over small caption
4. **Inline phone capture** — a card with a car photo, "SUR QUEL NUMÉRO JE PEUX TE
   JOINDRE ?", phone input with flag, full-width blue button, "100% confidentiel".
   A micro-conversion placed a third of the way down instead of a second CTA.
5. **"RÉSULTATS réels"** — split proof: left column is three **9:16 vertical**
   video testimonials with durations and italic blue captions; right column is
   three real Shopify dashboard screenshots showing €73,320 / €8,960 / €32,458.
   Video proof + data proof side by side.
6. **Logo marquee** — two infinite tickers scrolling in *opposite* directions
   (Shopify, TikTok Ads, Stripe, Meta Ads, Klaviyo, Google Ads, Instagram,
   YouTube, Analytics, PayPal, Mailchimp). Borrowed credibility from tools, not clients.
7. **"TU POSTES. Rien ne vend."** — problem agitation, four bordered pill rows
8. **"3 PILIERS"** — three numbered cards (01/02/03) with blue circular icons:
   Offre irrésistible / Acquisition rentable / Scaling maîtrisé
9. **Written testimonials** — 2 rows × 3 cards, 5 blue stars each, quote, name, role
10. **Stat bar** — 4-up: +270 / +3M€ / 4.9-5 satisfaction / 100% concret
11. **"HISTOIRE SANS FILTRE"** — founder story as two-column editorial: a candid
    street portrait, long personal copy, then a second photo (watch/jewelry).
    Deliberately raw photography, not studio — reads as authentic rather than staged.
12. **"ZÉRO ENGAGEMENT. ZÉRO PRESSION."** — de-risking closer
13. **Footer** — one line, copyright + legal links

### CTA cadence
A blue pill CTA closes *every single section* — roughly 7 of them, each with
different copy escalating in commitment ("Je réserve mon appel gratuit" → "Je veux
la solution" → "Je passe à l'action" → "Je rejoins les +270 élèves" → "Raconte-moi
ton histoire"). The button copy tracks the reader's emotional state at that point
in the page. Excellent.

### Mobile
- Probably designed mobile-first. The 9:16 testimonial videos are natively phone-shaped.
- Stat pairs → side-by-side still works at 2-up; the 4-up bar goes 2×2
- 3-column testimonial grid → single column or swipe carousel
- Logo marquee is already a ticker — best-case mobile element, no adaptation needed
- Founder story stacks photo-then-text cleanly
- Mixed caps/italic headline survives scaling because the two type styles stay
  distinguishable at small sizes
- Perspective grid in hero is a CSS gradient, cheap on mobile

### Verdict
The most sophisticated design of the four. Best typography, best rhythm, best CTA
strategy, most convincing proof stack. If you want a page that looks expensive,
this is the template.

## Reference 3 — NetRevenue

**Category:** Corporate/SaaS-styled sales agency. Sells credibility, not urgency.

### Palette
- Canvas: dark charcoal (`~#0A0A0C`)
- Accent 1: violet/purple (`~#7C5CFF`) — glows, nav pill, headline highlight
- Accent 2: orange (`~#FF8A3D`) — star ratings and a highlighted word per heading
- The only two-accent page of the four
- Large soft purple radial glows behind hero and final CTA

### Typography
- **Title case, not uppercase** — "Scale The Real Potential of Your Sales Team".
  Clean geometric sans (Inter / Satoshi family). No condensed display face,
  no italic serif.
- This alone makes it read corporate rather than "internet marketer"

### Section-by-section
1. **Nav** — floating rounded glass pill with a light border. Logo left, Careers
   + Case Studies center, lavender "Become a partner" pill right.
2. **Hero** — eyebrow "IT IS SIMPLE - WE HELP YOU MAKE MORE MONEY", title-case
   headline with "The Real Potential" in purple, subhead, white pill CTA
3. **Hero media** — a large rounded card showing the founder in front of a branded
   slide reading "NET REVENUE $288,000,000". The proof number is baked into the
   image, so it lands before any copy is read.
4. **Customer Testimonials** — carousel of cards, 5 orange stars, quote, a "See
   video testimonial" link, avatar + name + company. Dot pagination plus circular
   prev/next arrows at bottom right.
5. **"Meet Your Executive Team"** — bento-style asymmetric photo grid, 3 large
   cards on top, 2 below, each a portrait with name + C-suite title overlaid.
   Naming a CEO/CTO/CRO is a deliberate enterprise signal.
6. **"DOCUMENTED RESULTS"** — 7 case-study cards in a 3-column grid. Each has a
   **light** dashboard/chart screenshot at the top, a specific headline ("How We
   Fixed the Margins and Broke the Plateau..."), a paragraph, and "Read more →".
   The light screenshots against the dark card create the page's strongest visual pop.
7. **"Our System to Automate Your Sales"** — oversized ghosted heading on the left,
   a vertical connected timeline on the right with three stages (Consulting /
   Build Out / Operations), each an icon in a circle plus 3-4 bullets
8. **Testimonial spotlight** — single large quote with photo, orange stars,
   named attribution with a hard number ("closed more than five million dollars...
   average ticket around $9,600")
9. **Final CTA** — rounded panel with purple glow, "Ready to Transform Your Sales
   Game?", light pill button
10. **Footer** — logo left, five social icons right

Note: the column of small blue icons down the right edge is almost certainly a
page-builder editing artifact, not part of the design.

### Mobile
- Floating pill nav → hamburger inside the same pill
- Hero card holds up well full-width; the "$288,000,000" text stays legible
- Testimonial carousel is already swipe-native
- Bento team grid → 1 or 2 columns; the asymmetry is lost, which costs it some
  visual interest
- **Biggest mobile risk:** 7 case-study cards at 1 column is a very long scroll,
  and the dashboard screenshots inside them become unreadable thumbnails. Needs
  "show more" pagination and cropped/zoomed screenshot variants.
- The timeline section is naturally vertical → best mobile section on the page
- Purple radial glows are cheap CSS gradients, no performance issue

### Verdict
The most trustworthy-feeling of the four and the least aggressive. Title case,
named executives, and documented case studies target a buyer who's suspicious of
hype. Weakest on urgency — there is no scarcity, no deadline, no price anywhere.

## Reference 4 — B2C Growth (cold email agency)

**Category:** Modern agency template. Structurally the closest fit to a sales-agency site.

### Palette
- Canvas: black with a faint red-warm tint
- Accent: crimson (`~#E11D2E`) with heavy red radial bloom behind the hero video
- Cards: dark panels with subtle 1px borders, slight red tint on hover-state styling

### Typography
- Title case sans (Inter-ish) + **italic serif** on the accent word of every
  heading — same device as Reference 2:
  - "Start Signing Clients" + "*On-Demand*"
  - "Our Client " + "*Results*"
  - "How much revenue you're " + "*missing*" + " each month"
  - "Fix that in just " + "*90 days*"
  - "What " + "*Clients*" + " Say"
  - "Frequently Asked " + "*Questions*"

### Section-by-section
1. **Nav** — floating pill, "b2cgrowth" logo, six links (Home, Clients, About Us,
   Blog, Testimonials, Guides), red "Book a call →" pill
2. **Scarcity eyebrow** — small red dot + "NOW BOOKING - Q1"
3. **Hero** — mixed-type headline, subhead, video card whose thumbnail reads "sign
   clients on-demand" with a **circular founder headshot inset bottom-right**
   (picture-in-picture) and a mouse cursor graphic. Big red bloom behind it.
4. **CTA with scarcity** — "Book a Call + 3 Spots Left for Q1"
5. **"Our Client Results"** — 3 bordered stat cards: $9.5M revenue generated /
   13.9K+ meetings booked / 99.9% qualified meetings
6. **"Our KPIs"** — 3 more, and these are the smarter ones because they're
   operational rather than vanity: $2.7 cost per positive reply / $0.5-$2 cost per
   meeting / 3-13% reply rate. Publishing unit economics reads as confidence.
7. **Guides banner** — a strip promoting playbooks with an "Explore Guides →" pill
   (content/SEO capture for people not ready to book)
8. **ROI calculator** — the standout element. An interactive card with three
   inputs (Offer Price 3000, Close Rate 30%, Meetings/Month 80) computing
   "Potential Income $72,000" in large red. The visitor calculates their own gap,
   so the number is self-generated and therefore believed.
9. **"Fix that in just 90 days"** — 3 staggered cards along a vertical connector
   line with red nodes: Full Setup / Active Campaigns / Transition
10. **"Meet the Founder"** — portrait in a rounded card with red glow, copy right,
    red "Connect on LinkedIn" + outlined "Follow on Instagram"
11. **"What Clients Say"** — two large video testimonial cards with YouTube-style
    thumbnails showing transformations ("$7K → $35K/MO"), plus "View All
    Testimonials" / "View Case Studies" buttons
12. **FAQ** — accordion, ~10 questions with + expanders, handling objections
    (pricing, timeline, onboarding, domain reputation, guarantees)
13. **Final CTA** + footer with logo/description, MENU and RESOURCES columns,
    socials, legal

Note: the white horizontal bands between sections are stitching seams from the
screenshot capture, not design elements.

### Mobile
- Pill nav → hamburger; six links is too many for a mobile bar
- Hero video card + red bloom is the strongest mobile moment
- 3-up stat rows → likely 3 stacked or a 2+1; numbers stay large and readable
- **The ROI calculator is the mobile risk and the mobile opportunity.** Number
  inputs and sliders on a phone need large tap targets, a numeric keypad, and a
  result that stays visible while the user edits. Done right it's the highest-
  engagement element on the page; done lazily it's three fiddly inputs and a
  keyboard covering the answer.
- Staggered 90-day timeline → straight vertical stack
- Founder section stacks photo-over-copy
- Video testimonial thumbnails with big text ("$7K → $35K/MO") read perfectly on
  mobile — the text is baked into the image at a size that survives scaling
- FAQ accordion is ideal mobile UX (collapsed by default = short scroll)

### Verdict
The most directly transferable structure. Same shape of business (agency, paid on
performance, sells a sales outcome), and its two best ideas — publishing unit
economics and the self-serve ROI calculator — both suit a commission-only model.

## Cross-cutting patterns

All four pages share a formula:

| Element | All four do this |
|---|---|
| Background | Dark. Black or near-black. Zero exceptions. |
| Accent color | Exactly ONE saturated accent (Ref 3 stretches to two). Used for eyebrows, one highlighted word per heading, buttons, icons, stat numbers, glows. Nothing else is colored. |
| Above the fold | A VSL with an oversized play button. All four. |
| Section rhythm | Tiny letterspaced eyebrow → headline with one accent word → subhead → content → CTA. Repeated identically down the page. |
| Proof | Front-loaded and numeric. Revenue figures, client counts, dashboards, star ratings. |
| Alignment | Center-aligned, narrow content column inside a wide dark canvas. |
| CTA | One single action repeated 4-8 times, never competing offers. |
| Founder | A face. All four put a specific human on the page. |

### Where they diverge

- **Ref 1** is direct response: inline order form, value stacking, struck-through
  pricing, disclaimer wall. Highest pressure, lowest perceived prestige.
- **Ref 2** is editorial: best typography, strongest rhythm, most authentic proof.
- **Ref 3** is corporate: title case, named executives, written case studies, zero
  urgency. Highest trust, lowest push.
- **Ref 4** is the modern agency template: scarcity, unit economics, interactive
  calculator, FAQ objection handling.

### Techniques worth stealing

1. **Heavy sans + italic serif headline pairing** (Refs 2 and 4) — the current
   look in this niche, and it costs nothing to implement
2. **CTA copy that escalates with the scroll** (Ref 2) — the button says what the
   reader is ready to do at that point, not the same words seven times
3. **Interactive ROI calculator** (Ref 4) — the prospect generates the number
   themselves, so they believe it
4. **Publishing unit economics** (Ref 4) — cost per meeting, reply rate. Only a
   confident operator shows these.
5. **Dual proof columns** (Ref 2) — video testimonials beside raw dashboard
   screenshots
6. **Opposite-direction logo marquees** (Ref 2) — borrowed credibility, zero
   mobile cost
7. **Baking the headline number into the video thumbnail** (Ref 3's $288,000,000,
   Ref 4's "$7K → $35K/MO") — proof that lands before any copy is read
8. **A full-bleed inverted CTA block** (Ref 1's red section) — one total contrast
   break the reader cannot scroll past
9. **FAQ accordion as objection handling** (Ref 4) — short on mobile, catches the
   hesitations a sales page can't address inline

### Recurring mobile failure modes to avoid

- Multi-column card grids collapsing to 1 column and creating dead scroll (Ref 1's
  9 cards, Ref 3's 7 case studies) — use 2 columns or progressive disclosure
- Screenshots inside cards becoming unreadable — crop to the number that matters
- Nav bars with 6+ links — needs a hamburger with a persistent CTA pill outside it
- Long inline forms in the hero pushing all proof below 3 screens of scroll
- Interactive inputs where the mobile keyboard covers the result

## Daniel G / WCC — above the fold, measured

Re-analysed because our own fold kept reading as "off". The proportions are the
whole trick, and they are nothing like a typical SaaS hero.

Reading down the first screen:

1. **Thin nav**, full width, small type. Logo left, two links centre, red pill
   right. It takes maybe 6% of the fold and gets out of the way.
2. **A headline that fills the width.** "WCC CERTIFIED" — *two words* — set in a
   heavy condensed uppercase face at roughly 8–9% of viewport width per
   character. It is far and away the largest thing on the page. The product
   name IS the headline; the promise lives in the subhead.
3. **A circular certification seal** overlapping the headline's top-right,
   breaking the type's bounding box. Credential as ornament.
4. **A small subhead, two lines**, immediately under the headline with very
   little gap. Perhaps a seventh the size of the headline. Formula:
   *"How to [stop pain], [stop pain], and [outcome] within [timeframe].
   [Risk reversal]."*
5. **A tiny uppercase video label** with a red play triangle:
   "▶ WATCH THIS TWO-MINUTE OVERVIEW VIDEO BELOW".
6. **The video, which is not large** — roughly 40% of viewport width, well
   under half the fold's height.

### The two ratios that matter

- **Headline to subhead is about 7:1.** Enormous display, small supporting
  copy. Most heroes run 3:1 and read timid by comparison.
- **The video is smaller than the headline block.** Our build had this
  inverted — an oversized video panel with a modest headline, which is exactly
  why it read as off. The headline is the hook; the video is the proof that
  follows it.

### Everything is tightly stacked

Gaps between headline, subhead, label and video are small. There is no
breathing room in the reference at all — the fold is dense on purpose, so the
whole pitch lands in one screen without scrolling.

### Background

A real photograph — a dark, blue-tinted room shot — pushed way down in
brightness. It supplies depth and a sense of place that no gradient does. With
no photograph available, the closest honest substitute is a lit-stage
treatment: a bright spotlight from top centre falling off into a heavy vignette
at the edges, so the fold reads as a lit environment rather than a flat panel.
